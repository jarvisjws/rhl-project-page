A failed robot execution can expose a weakness in the procedure used to solve
a task, rather than in the task specification itself. Robotic Heuristic
Learning (RHL) studies how a coding agent revises executable operational
knowledge from such feedback while preserving task requirements. The system
connects agent-led diagnosis and program editing to task-specific preparation,
robot execution and evaluation. Revised procedures can be retained and
instantiated from the state of later cases. We examine two simulated settings:
recovery from policy failures in RoboTwin and task-initial solving in RoboDojo.
Across the included development histories, the system attains the original
task goals in 104/115 eligible Twin cases and 620/627 cases in a fixed Dojo
batch. These are cumulative outcomes under changing versions and budgets;
Twin excludes six historically reviewed cases and Dojo membership is
outcome-selected. An audited interaction chain establishes agent-produced
revision followed by success. A separate geometric rule is retained and
consumed on 24 previously failed layouts, with 19 successes under expanded
horizons. Together, these results demonstrate a route from execution feedback
to task solutions and reusable operational knowledge. The present route
requires substantial iterative effort; comparative efficiency and broader
transfer remain unmeasured.
